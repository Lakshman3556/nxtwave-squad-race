### TEST 1 - Hook (Placement Proof vs. General Curiosity)
- **Hypothesis:** Final-year engineering students facing placement drives respond significantly better to an outcome-oriented, interview-proof hook ("Leave with an AI project you can demo in your next interview") than to a general curiosity hook ("Build your first AI project in 60 minutes").
- **What I would test:** I would test identical visual creative (poster) with the two headline variants across 4 comparable final-year branch/class WhatsApp groups each, while simultaneously splitting the ₹600 paid budget evenly (₹300 per variant) across two targeted ad variants on LinkedIn/Instagram.
- **Metric:** Link click-to-registration conversion rate per variant. I will declare a winning hook at a relative lift of ≥20% with a minimum sample size of 100 clicks per variant, and standardize on the winning hook for all messaging from Day 4 onward.

### TEST 2 - Social Motivation (College-vs-College Race vs. Individual-Only Rank)
- **Hypothesis:** A collegiate tribal identity ("Squad Race: My college is ranked #7, only 3 signups behind Vasavi") creates a stronger viral incentive to share on WhatsApp than an individual-only referral rank.
- **What I would test:** Post-registration A/B split on the Squad Card: 50% of registrants see the College Race Card (displaying campus rank, deficit to the next college, and campus signups), while 50% see an individual milestone card (displaying only personal referral counts).
- **Metric:** 24-hour WhatsApp share button click rate and referred registrations per registrant (k-factor). If the college race variant drives a relative increase in share rate of ≥25%, the collegiate mechanic remains enabled throughout the campaign.

### TEST 3 - Channel Trust & Sender Authority (Peer vs. Placement vs. Club)
